# Vibe Marketing First — The Alternative Startup Playbook

> **Don't learn to code. Learn to sell first.**

This is the companion repo for the article: [How to Build an App from Scratch — The Marketing-First Approach](https://axelfreeman.com/blog/vibe-coding-guide.html)

## The Core Idea

Everyone tells you: "Learn to code, build MVP, launch." This is backwards.

**Code has zero value in 2026.** Everyone can vibe code. The scarce resource isn't coding ability — it's the ability to exchange words, images, and videos for human attention.

## Three Mistakes → Three Rules

| Mistake | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Building for a market with no buyers | Validate demand with words before code |
| Building 60% of what the market needs | Ship marketing first, ship product second |
| Expecting users in month 1 | Plan for 6 months of marketing grind |

### Prompt 1: Validate Your Idea (The Brutal Truth)

```
You are an idea validator. Your job is NOT to be supportive — it's to be honest.

I have an idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR IDEA IN 2-3 SENTENCES]

Do the following:

1. SEARCH THE MARKET. Find real data on how much money is being spent in this market right now. Use multiple sources — industry reports, competitor pricing, public financials. Only use data from the last 6 months. Prioritize official or semi-official sources over blog posts.

2. MATH CHECK. From a pure business and mathematical perspective — can this idea make money? Calculate: average deal size × realistic conversion rate × addressable market = potential revenue. Be conservative.

3. SEPARATE REAL FROM SCAM. This market is full of projects that don't work and inflated expectations. Identify which part of this idea has real profit potential versus which part is wishful thinking. Call out the scam-like elements directly.

4. FIVE REASONS THIS WILL FAIL. Give me 5 specific, brutal reasons why this idea will NOT work. Not generic advice — reasons specific to this idea, this market, this moment.

5. TIMELINE REALITY CHECK. Whatever timeline I have in my head — double it. Then explain why. Use real examples of similar projects and how long they actually took.

6. SOLO FOUNDER ASSESSMENT. I'm a solo founder just starting out — learning vibe coding, business, and marketing all at once. Answer honestly:
   a) Can YOU realistically make money from this idea?
   b) If the idea fails — are the skills you'll learn monetizable elsewhere?
   c) Does investing time in this increase your hourly value in the job market?
   d) Is this market/niche NOT dying — does it have 5+ year potential? Or is it a trend that'll be gone next year?

7. FINAL VERDICT. One sentence: worth it or skip it. No hedging.
```

### Prompt 2: Write a Landing Page That Sells Before You Build

```
You are a direct-response copywriter. Your job is to sell the IDEA before the product exists.

My product idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT]

Write a landing page that follows these rules:

1. NO FEATURES. Don't list what the product does. Describe the TRANSFORMATION — what does the customer's life look like after using it?

2. LEAD WITH THE PROBLEM. First paragraph: describe the pain so accurately the reader says "that's exactly me."

3. UNDER 200 WORDS. Every word must earn its place. Cut fluff.

4. ONE CALL TO ACTION. One button. One action. No choices.

5. SOCIAL PROOF PLACEHOLDER. Insert "[Real customer quote here]" — this forces you to think about what a satisfied user would actually say.

6. READ IT ALOUD TEST. Write like you talk. If you wouldn't say it to a friend over coffee, rewrite it.
```

### Prompt 3: Generate Content That Attracts Attention

```
You are a growth marketer. Write content to attract people to a product that doesn't exist yet.

My product idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT]
My target audience: [WHO ARE THEY]

Generate:

1. 5 TWEETS — each targeting a different emotional trigger: frustration with current solutions, curiosity about a better way, social proof ("look what this person achieved"), contrarian take ("why the standard advice is wrong"), and a direct ask ("who else is struggling with this?").

2. 1 REDDIT POST — format: "I built X. Here's what I learned." Focus on the JOURNEY, not the product. Share mistakes, surprises, and one counter-intuitive insight. End with: "Would love feedback from people who've solved similar problems."

3. 1 LINKEDIN POST — professional but vulnerable. Talk about the PROBLEM you observed, why existing solutions suck, and what you're building differently. No pitch — just the mission.

4. HOOKS ONLY — give me 10 one-sentence hooks for social media. The best hooks either: reveal something surprising, promise a specific result, or attack a common belief.
```

### Prompt 4: Find Your First 10 Users (Without Spending Money)

```
You are a community strategist. Help me find my first 10 users without paid ads.

My product: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT]
My target user: [WHO IS YOUR IDEAL USER]

Do the following:

1. WHERE DO THEY HANG OUT? Search for the specific subreddits, Discord servers, Telegram channels, Slack communities, and Twitter/X circles where my target users are active. Be specific — name the communities, not generic categories.

2. WHAT DO THEY COMPLAIN ABOUT? Find 5 real complaints or frustrations my target users are posting about right now. Quote them directly. The best first users come from solving an active pain, not introducing a new concept.

3. WHAT SHOULD MY FIRST POST SAY? Write my introduction post. Rules: no pitch, no link, no "check out my product." Just contribute value — share an insight, answer a question, or point out something nobody noticed. People buy from people they recognize, not strangers with links.

4. THE DM STRATEGY. After I've posted and contributed value for a week, how do I reach out to people directly? Write 3 DM templates: one for someone who engaged with my post, one for someone complaining about the problem I solve, and one for a potential collaborator.

5. MEASUREMENT. How do I know if this is working? Define 3 metrics that matter more than "number of users" — engagement, conversations started, referrals. Numbers I can track without analytics tools.
```
