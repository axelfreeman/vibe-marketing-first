# Vibe Marketing First — The Alternative Startup Playbook

> **Don't learn to code. Learn to sell first.**

This is the companion repo for the article: [How to Build an App from Scratch — The Marketing-First Approach](https://axelfreeman.com/blog/vibe-coding-guide.html)

## The Core Idea

Everyone tells you: "Learn to code, build MVP, launch." This is backwards.

**Code has zero value in 2026.** Everyone can vibe code. The scarce resource isn't coding ability — it's the ability to exchange words, images, and videos for human attention.

## Three Mistakes → Three Rules

| Mistake | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Building for a market with no buyers | Validate demand with words before code |
| Building 60% of what the market needs | Ship marketing first, ship product second |
| Expecting users in month 1 | Plan for 6 months of marketing grind |

### Prompt 1: Validate Your Idea (The Brutal Truth)

```
You are an idea validator. Your job is NOT to be supportive — it's to be honest.

I have an idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR IDEA IN 2-3 SENTENCES]

Do the following:

1. SEARCH THE MARKET. Find real data on how much money is being spent in this market right now. Use multiple sources — industry reports, competitor pricing, public financials. Only use data from the last 6 months. Prioritize official or semi-official sources over blog posts.

2. MATH CHECK. From a pure business and mathematical perspective — can this idea make money? Calculate: average deal size × realistic conversion rate × addressable market = potential revenue. Be conservative.

3. SEPARATE REAL FROM SCAM. This market is full of projects that don't work and inflated expectations. Identify which part of this idea has real profit potential versus which part is wishful thinking. Call out the scam-like elements directly.

4. FIVE REASONS THIS WILL FAIL. Give me 5 specific, brutal reasons why this idea will NOT work. Not generic advice — reasons specific to this idea, this market, this moment.

5. TIMELINE REALITY CHECK. Whatever timeline I have in my head — double it. Then explain why. Use real examples of similar projects and how long they actually took.

6. SOLO FOUNDER ASSESSMENT. I'm a solo founder just starting out — learning vibe coding, business, and marketing all at once. Answer honestly:
   a) Can YOU realistically make money from this idea?
   b) If the idea fails — are the skills you'll learn monetizable elsewhere?
   c) Does investing time in this increase your hourly value in the job market?
   d) Is this market/niche NOT dying — does it have 5+ year potential? Or is it a trend that'll be gone next year?

7. FINAL VERDICT. One sentence: worth it or skip it. No hedging.
```

### Prompt 2: Find Your Audience & Where They Buy

```
You are a market intelligence analyst. I have an idea for a product. If I don't provide enough detail, ask me to clarify before proceeding.

My idea: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT]

Do the following — use real data from your searches, not assumptions:

1. WHO ALREADY PAYS FOR THIS? Find who is already spending money on solutions like mine. Don't guess — search for actual buyers. Look at competitor customer lists, reviews, testimonials, case studies. Identify repeating audience segments.

2. AUDIENCE PORTRAITS. Based on real data, describe 3-5 distinct audience portraits. For each:
   - Who they are (role, company type, demographics)
   - What problem they're solving (in their own words, quote them if possible)
   - How much they're paying right now for alternatives
   - Where they discover new products (Google search? AI tools? Communities? Ads?)

3. WHERE ARE THEIR COMMUNITIES? List the specific places this audience gathers: subreddits, Discord servers, Telegram channels, Slack groups, industry forums, Twitter/X circles. Don't name categories — name actual communities.

4. HOW DO THEY SEARCH? Do they Google their problems or ask AI agents (ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek)? This determines your entire marketing strategy — SEO for Google, AEO for AI agents, or both.

5. COMPETITOR TRAFFIC SOURCES. Search the internet for ad cases, marketing breakdowns, or public revenue data from companies doing the same or similar thing. Answer:
   - Where do they get their traffic? Social media? Influencers? Google Ads? SEO? Communities?
   - If they use influencers/creators — which ones? How much do those creators charge?
   - If they use Google Ads — find any available CPC estimates, conversion rates, or budget benchmarks
   - If they use communities — which ones? What's their engagement strategy?

6. REALISTIC COMMUNICATION PLAN. Based on everything above, write a step-by-step plan:
   - Channel 1: [name] — expected cost: [estimate], expected time to first result: [estimate]
   - Channel 2: [name] — expected cost: [estimate], expected time to first result: [estimate]
   - Channel 3: [name] — expected cost: [estimate], expected time to first result: [estimate]
   - First action to take TODAY (not "plan more" — a concrete step)

7. BUDGET REALITY CHECK. Based on competitor data and market norms, what's a realistic monthly marketing budget for the first 3 months? Don't give me "it depends" — give me a range with reasons.
```

### Prompt 3: Write Your Sales Proposal — Short, Medium, Long

```
You are a sales strategist. Based on everything we've already analyzed — the market data, competitor research, audience portraits, and traffic sources — write a sales proposal for my product.

My product: [DESCRIBE YOUR PRODUCT IN ONE SENTENCE]

Scan what we've already learned from the previous prompts. Look at how competitors sell their offers — their wording, their hooks, their angles. Use that as reference.

Give me THREE versions of the same sales pitch, at different lengths:

**SHORT — 100 characters**
For Telegram DMs, push notifications, text messages, Twitter bio. Must stop the scroll and make someone want to learn more in one breath.

**MEDIUM — 300 characters**
For LinkedIn DMs, cold emails, Instagram posts, community introductions. Who it's for, what problem it solves, why it's different, what to do next.

**LONG — 800 characters**
For landing pages, pitch decks, partnership proposals. Full value proposition: the pain → the current alternatives (and why they suck) → your solution → the transformation → social proof hook → call to action.

For each version:
- Write like a human, not a marketer. No "revolutionary," no "game-changing," no "cutting-edge."
- Make it specific. Replace generic claims with concrete promises.
- Include the core emotional trigger that makes someone say "I need this."

When you're done, tell me:
1. Which competitor's sales approach this most closely matches
2. What's missing that would make it stronger
3. One question I should answer to improve the pitch
```

### Prompt 4: Find Your First Users — From 1 to 10

```
You are a user acquisition strategist. Your first job is simple:

ASK: Are you using your own product?

If NO → Stop. Go use it. The first user is found — it's you. Come back when you've used your product for at least a week and can describe what's good and what's broken from personal experience. No exceptions.

If YES → Good. User #1 is you. Now let's find users #2 through #10.

Here's the hard truth: on any market big enough to matter, you CAN steal 9 users from competitors. Don't tell me you can't — I won't believe you. The market is large enough, and competitors are slow enough, that 9 people will switch if you give them a reason.

Now, based on everything we already know — the audience portraits, where they hang out, what competitors sell, their traffic sources — generate a "STEAL 9 USERS" playbook:

1. BRAND HIJACKING. List your top 3 competitors by name. For each, suggest:
   - Which brand comparison pages you should create ("Product X vs Product Y")
   - Which keywords to bid on in Google Ads (brand names, "alternatives to X," "X vs")
   - Which review/listing sites the competitor appears on where you don't

2. COMMUNITY SNIPING. Based on the communities we identified earlier:
   - Find 3 specific posts where people are complaining about a competitor
   - Write a response to each — not a pitch, but a helpful comment that reveals you exist
   - What's the one feature or angle that makes you the obvious alternative?

3. DIRECT POACHING. 
   - Find 5 people who publicly tweeted or posted about using a competitor
   - Write a DM template that says: "Saw you use X. What's the one thing you wish it did better?" — no pitch, just research
   - When they answer, your product should do that thing

4. DISTRIBUTION HACKS.
   - List 3 directories, marketplaces, or app stores where competitors are listed. What's the process to get listed there?
   - What integrations does the competitor have that you could piggyback on? (e.g., "they have a Slack integration → you should too")
   - Is there a free tool, template, or resource you can give away that your competitor charges for?

5. THE 9-USER TIMELINE.
   - Week 1: actions to take
   - Week 2: actions to take
   - Week 3: actions to take
   - By end of week 3, you have 9 users or you're doing something wrong.

OUTPUT RULE: No theory. No "consider doing X." Every item must be a specific, executable action with a competitor name, a platform name, or a community name attached.
```
